The studies

S1

Goal Visibility

formerly the density study · 12–19 Oct 2026Done · L3 on hold

What each interface sees at a goal, by goal geometry and city clutter; it picked the trial levels by a rule fixed in advance.

Limits

  • Scripted single-drone policies under-predict real pilots about 2.7×, so the small-goal advantage is an upper bound.
  • Its "What each sees" sweep widths saturate for the swarm (found in S3).
Explorerdocs/studies/S1_goal_visibility.md

Findings

  • At a goal, its geometry decides who sees more and clutter barely matters: swarm − single exposure +0.048 at today's 7 m occluder, +0.106 at a 28 m one, −0.047 at a cramped goal the formation cannot surround.
  • Trial levels L1 C0-G0, L2 C0-G10, L3 C2-G7. On pilot paths L3 loses 2.99 drones per run against a bar of 2.5, mostly at spread 0.4.
  • Predicted discovery time of an unannounced goal, swarm ÷ single: 0.58–0.69 on shown feeds.
S2

Hidden Goal Search

formerly the search study · 15–16 Oct 2026Done

How soon each interface finds a goal patch whose position it does not know, flown by a random walker and by internal_3's fitted pilots.

Limits

  • People found goals 2.3× (swarm) and 10× (single drone) faster than the fitted pilot, so the human advantage may be smaller.
  • Counting only the head's view, the advantage shrinks to 0.81–1.03.
Results dashboard (light rebuild)docs/studies/S2_hidden_goal_search.md

Findings

  • The swarm finds a hidden goal 6–18% sooner (restricted mean time ratio 0.82–0.94, random walker; 0.77–0.92 fitted pilot).
  • It is the cameras, not the morphing: a swarm steered like the single drone does as well, except in the packed city, where steering splits it.
  • At spread 0.4 the swarm is slower than one drone in dense cities (1.12–1.34) and loses half its searches.
S3

City Roaming

formerly the clutter study · 20–21 Oct 2026Done · refined re-run proposed

What each interface sees on task-free walks across 63 Voronoi city designs (block size × building coverage), at spreads 0.4, 1.0 and 1.6, and where spreading stops helping.

Limits

  • Its sweep width capped at 84 m and understated the swarm; fixed in code, not yet re-flown.
  • Its hidden-target measure saturates and is dominated by the view from the start; refinements proposed.
Results pageS3 report (for VoronoiCityWorld)docs/studies/S3_city_roaming.md

Findings

  • The swarm is ahead on every measure, in all 63 designs, at every spread (q < 0.05).
  • Its lead in finding a hidden target grows with building coverage: 1.48× at 0.02 to 3.5× at 0.14 (spread 1.0); block size barely matters.
  • Spread 1.6 beats 1.0 (11% faster coverage), 0.4 is worst everywhere and loses 4.8 drones per walk; wider spreads lose screen-to-screen overlap.
S4

Split and Surround

the split study · 27–28 Oct 2026Done

In a city of small blocks with one large building each, can the swarm surround a block, show the pilot every street round it, and reform down the one chosen quickly enough to explore block by block?

Limits

  • Only the smallest blocks (40 m, buildings about 13 m across) passed Phase 1, so Phase 2 measured the regime S2 did; at larger blocks the surround needs 9–20 s or more and the pass round strands a drone.
  • Scripted pilots and one 6 s surround; on head-ahead knowledge alone the surround leaned slower (×1.16).
Results pageCities and flightsLook down (Phase 3)docs/studies/S4_split_and_surround.md

Findings

  • Surrounding a block shows the pilot more of its streets: 95.5% at the decision, 7.1 points more than a pause of the same length (H1).
  • It finds a hidden target no sooner than the swarm kept in the streets (×1.02, 0.92–1.12; H2 not supported): it saves 10% of the blocks searched, and each block takes 21% longer.
  • The cameras are the effect, as in S2: the swarm in the streets finds a target in 62% of one drone's time. Surrounding costs about ten times the drones lost, and a quarter of its screens share nothing with a neighbour.
  • Phase 3, a short pilot: scored on looking down each street to the building at its end, the swarm in the streets knows 41% of a block's exits at 40 m blocks (the old preview said 71%), and a 12 s surround looks down 93%, more the wider d_ref, up to where its drones touch the building (1.8, past the trials' dial). Searching for a goal at a building, the surround still took ×1.48 the street-bound swarm's time.
  • Its page shows every design's city and replays 73 of its flights, Unity's beside the replica's.

Lessons carried forward

The full list, with what to do instead, is docs/studies/lessons.md. A new study should work through it before its design is fixed.

Measures

  1. Check every measure for a ceiling. Sweep width capped at 84 m and hid spread 1.6's reach. S3
  2. Compare rates or times, not a chance within a fixed time, which saturates. S3
  3. Hide targets out of sight of the start, or the view from the start dominates. S2 S3
  4. Report range and attention as dimensions: 40, 80 and 200 m; any screen, head ahead, fitted head. S1 S2 S3
  5. Separate the goal from the transit: geometry rules one, coverage the other. S1 S3
  6. Seeing more is not finding sooner until a pilot acts on it; split the result into decisions × time per decision. S4
  7. Score a view by the decision it informs: seeing the start of a street is not seeing down it to the building at its end. S4
  8. Score each condition at its own decision: a pass decides at the corner, before it goes round. S4

Models and statistics

  1. Scripted pilots are not people; calibrate against a pilot block. S1 S2
  2. Spread 0.4 loses drones in every model; report per spread, consider a dial floor. S1 S2 S3
  3. Validate the replica in each new kind of city with a Unity spot check. S1 S3 S4
  4. Cities are the statistical units; tests over walks overstate. S3
  5. Pair every condition on one intent chain from one start. S2 S3
  6. Size a pass/fail run to its bar, then confirm on fresh trials in fresh cities. S4
  7. Read the full run, not the first rounds: S4's H2 read ×1.12–1.39 early and ×1.02 in the end. S4
  8. Check an adaptive pilot for dead ends, and a walk for early ends, before the run. S4

Manoeuvres and tuning

  1. A stronger long-range attraction can raise building contacts: it drags drones across a block. S4
  2. A manoeuvre's timing scales with its geometry; one length for every design fits only the small blocks. S4
  3. Look past a censored recovery measure before reading it. S4
  4. Screen a mitigation against the failure it might cause. S4
  5. Treat the separation (d_ref) as a factor, sized to the task: looking down a block paid for spreads past d* and past the trials' dial. S4

Running on this machine

  1. The CPU faults: dead-worker recovery, retries, a bit-for-bit recheck, E-core pinning. S1 S2 S3
  2. Make long runs resumable, replicate by replicate, under a time budget. S3
  3. Never edit modules a running pool imports. S3
  4. Subset scenarios by index, never by drawing fewer. S2
  5. Never rewrite a script a stopped process may still be reading. S4

Reporting

  1. Every study's page has a Cities tab: its cities drawn, and its own flights to replay, each re-flown and checked identical to the run. A standing requirement. S4
  2. Define every code in plain words where a reader first meets it: a key under the masthead, hover definitions, a Terms tab. S4
  3. Check a claim about why something happens against the events it is about: S4's replays showed B's contacts were with the building being passed, not across the street. Know what a counter counts, too. S4

Starting a new study

  1. Read docs/studies/lessons.md and the entries of the studies it builds on; say which lessons apply and how each is handled.
  2. Take the next ID (S5), copy the template in docs/studies/README.md to docs/studies/S5_<slug>.md and add it to the table there.
  3. Keep the detailed manual in docs/<code_name>/README.md and outputs in experiment_results/<code_name>/.
  4. Give its page a Cities tab and a key to its codes: the cities drawn, and representative flights of every condition replayed (citysim/playback.py, replay_view.js; S4's page is the example). Choose the flights in the design.
  5. When it finishes, fill in its findings and refinements, add new lessons, and add its card here (source: docs/studies/hub.html).
