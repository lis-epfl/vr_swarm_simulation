--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. THE FRAMING DIFFERENCE THAT GOVERNS EVERYTHING ELSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both papers are INCREMENTAL GEOREFERENCED MOSAICKING: images arrive
asynchronously, coverage grows over minutes, the output is a map, and being
complete at any given instant is not a requirement. MGRAPH is explicit --
out-of-order injection is its headline feature. SkyEye's reference-frame scheme
is an amortization over a video that keeps arriving.

PLANAR is LIVE VIEW SYNTHESIS: every frame, exactly the currently-selected N
views, into a fixed canvas framed on a nominated centre drone, at 20 Hz, for an
HMD. Nothing persists. No map, no accumulation, no out-of-order arrival, no
reference frame to drift from.

That difference is why most of the machinery in both papers is inapplicable, and
why the parts that ARE applicable sit in the correction layer, never the
backbone. It also sets the standard both papers can be held to: a mapping
product can drop a bad frame because a later frame fills in. A live single-frame
HMD view has no later frame. Any mechanism borrowed from these papers that
DISCARDS data has to be re-justified against that, and mostly cannot be.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. AXIS-BY-AXIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WARP SOURCE
  PLANAR   Closed form from K, R, C, plane:  G = K R [e1 | e2 | (O - C)]
  SkyEye   Sensor rotation for SHAPE only; vision supplies position and scale
  MGRAPH   Vision only (ORB -> RANSAC); GPS used solely to prune candidates

DEGREES OF FREEDOM
  PLANAR   Full 8-DoF projective, geometrically exact
  SkyEye   Orthorectify, then 4-DoF similarity
  MGRAPH   4-DoF similarity (s, alpha, dx, dy)

CORRESPONDENCES
  PLANAR   NONE in the backbone
  SkyEye   SIFT + RANSAC + oRANSAC, every frame
  MGRAPH   ORB + BF-Hamming + ratio filter + RANSAC

REFERENCE VIEW
  PLANAR   None -- every view is absolute w.r.t. the plane frame
  SkyEye   Per-stream reference frame, updated on drift / sensor change
  MGRAPH   Per-graph reference vertex; absolute H by cascading strong edges

GLOBAL CONSISTENCY
  PLANAR   Nothing to enforce -- no cascade, therefore no drift
  SkyEye   Puzzle-stitching greedy ordering by confidence
  MGRAPH   Ceres bundle adjustment over absolute homographies

PLANE ASSUMPTION
  PLANAR   ARBITRARY plane (facade or nadir), published per frame
  SkyEye   Horizontal ground, implied by gravity-referenced orthorectification
  MGRAPH   Horizontal ground, altitude >> relief, gyrostabilized nadir

LOW-TEXTURE BEHAVIOUR
  PLANAR   Backbone unaffected; only the estimators lose signal
  SkyEye   Documented failure (7.3.6(4): deserts, calm lakes)
  MGRAPH   Documented failure -- image dropped below keypoint threshold

OBLIQUITY
  PLANAR   Exact at any angle
  SkyEye   Sensor path invariant; vision path collapses by 30-40 deg
  MGRAPH   Assumes near-nadir

BLENDING
  PLANAR   Voronoi-by-obliquity x analytic border feather, or cross-fade
  SkyEye   Not addressed
  MGRAPH   SKIPPED ENTIRELY -- GIS overlay, artifacts accepted

POSE / VIDEO SYNC
  PLANAR   DETECTED AND BOUNDED, NEVER CORRECTED. MAX_CAPTURE_SKEW_S drops a
           view lagging the freshest one; it does not synchronize anything, and
           CLAUDE.md notes skew COMMON to every aircraft passes through it
           untouched.
  SkyEye   Not addressed
  MGRAPH   Not addressed

COST
  PLANAR   The geometric solve is microseconds, but that is not the frame cost.
           _render also runs _nearest_weights ([N,HW] float32 temporaries, ~30 MB
           apiece at 8 views by its own comment) and _overlap_psnr, which is
           O(N^2) with TWO .item() GPU syncs per pair -- 56 stalls per frame at
           N=8 -- unconditionally. The number comparable to SkyEye's 8.8 fps is
           the ~21 Hz render loop, not the solve.
  SkyEye   8.8 fps at 5 streams (i7-10510U + MX250)
  MGRAPH   104 images in 32 s including optimization

Two consequences worth stating plainly.

(a) NEITHER PAPER'S METHOD WOULD WORK ON THE VERTICAL-FACADE CONFIGURATION AT
    ALL. SkyEye's orthorectification is defined against gravity -- the target is
    a top-down view and the accelerometer supplies the down vector -- so a
    90-degree off-nadir facade has no orthorectified view in its sense. MGRAPH's
    4-DoF similarity is justified explicitly by a gyrostabilized nadir camera.
    They are not competitors for the primary use case; they are component
    sources for the correction layer.

(b) MGRAPH's 7-11 px reprojection error is NOT a usable accuracy anchor for us,
    and rev 1 was wrong to treat it as one. It is the self-consistency residual
    of a 4-DoF similarity model fitted to ORB matches at 1000x750-1152x864 --
    not agreement with ground truth, not a seam displacement, and not at our
    focal length or resolution. A 4-DoF fit over real terrain with relief
    carries large residuals from un-modelled projective distortion that an
    8-DoF pose-parameterized model would not; their std EXCEEDING their mean
    says the distribution is outlier-dominated, so the mean is not a scale for a
    typical seam. No conclusion about what a vision front-end would achieve on
    the DJI path follows from that table.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. THE SkyEye SENSOR-BASED BASELINE SPECIFICALLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The closest thing in either paper to what PLANAR does, and instructive because
it is STRICTLY A SUBSET.

Their baseline is P_I = K [R|T] P_W with R from accelerometer + magnetometer
(Eq. 3), then M = R^-1 K^-1 to rectify. Then the decisive admission:

    "Due to the typically large data errors in GPS and barometer measurements,
     our method does not utilize these sensors, which means that after image
     orthorectification, we can only obtain the shape of the images but cannot
     determine their relative positional relationships and scaling factors."

So their baseline discards the POSITION half of the pose and the SCALE, handing
both to vision. PLANAR keeps both: metric camera centres plus an explicit plane
give absolute scale and absolute placement in one matmul, no reference view.
Structurally ours is the stronger formulation on identical sensors -- R^-1 K^-1
followed by a similarity search needs a reference and only works for a
horizontal plane; G needs neither.

The catch is that their reason for discarding GPS is exactly what CLAUDE.md
flags as the open question for the DJI path. Their Fig. 18(c) -- "approximately
correct image shapes, alignment compromised due to substantial GPS errors" -- is
a photograph of what our pose-only backbone produces on stock DJI telemetry, and
it matches the documented 20-60 px / 1-3.5 m budget. That is empirical
confirmation that the pose-only path degrades as predicted, and that vision is
what recovers it. It argues FOR a correction layer and AGAINST trusting raw pose
in the field -- not against the G parameterization.

Directly usable number: their Fig. 5 measures inlier count against pose error
(38 -> 24 -> 18 inliers at 0 / 10 / 20 degrees).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CLAUDE.md's error budget for a <=5 px seam at 30 m / 8 m baseline:

    plane distance          <= 1.07 m     <-- LEAST sensitive
    differential position   <= 0.29 m
    differential yaw        <= 0.55 deg
    gimbal pitch            <= 0.55 deg
    pose/video skew         ~55 px for 300 ms at the 40 deg/s yaw clamp
                                          <-- "larger than everything else
                                              combined"

Neither paper addresses skew at all. SkyEye's sensor confidence machinery
assumes the sensor sample and the frame describe the same instant; MGRAPH does
not discuss timing.

THAT IS THE RESULT OF THIS COMPARISON, not a gap to be filled with whatever the
papers happen to offer. Rev 1 ranked plane-distance bootstrap first -- the least
sensitive term -- listed sync as a PLANAR strength in the table and never
returned to it. Two of six rev-1 items addressed the plane; none addressed the
dominant term. That ranking is inverted and section 5 fixes it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. PLAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ordered by (error-budget weight) x (testable today). Everything below is
additive; nothing replaces a working path.

P1. MAKE SKEW OBSERVABLE.
    Highest budget weight, and neither paper helps -- so it is ours to build.
    Cannot be "fixed" today (the DJI path is still v1-header, no per-frame pose),
    but it can be MEASURED today, which is the actionable half. capture_time is
    already on the wire and already decoded by the staleness drop; report the
    skew DISTRIBUTION across views -- spread, and the common-mode component that
    MAX_CAPTURE_SKEW_S cannot see -- on the estimator log line rather than only
    gating on it. Cost: near zero, the data is in hand. Without this the field
    error budget's dominant term is the only one with no read-out at all.

P2. PER-VIEW GAIN COMPENSATION.
    The one rev-1 item to survive review intact, and the only one testable
    today. Independent auto-exposure across aircraft is guaranteed; under
    NEAREST an exposure mismatch is a brightness step at every winner boundary.
    _zero_mean protects the ESTIMATORS only -- _render and the PSNR diagnostic
    are unprotected.
    Three constraints, all learned from existing code rather than the papers:
      - Solve it on the warp thread's existing quarter-res pass, not in _render.
      - Measure over GEOMETRIC COVERAGE, not blend weight -- the same trap the
        overlap-PSNR comment already documents, since NEAREST makes the weights
        one-hot.
      - It has its own global gauge (a common gain on every view is free) and
        needs anchoring, like the refiner's zero-mean.
    Citation correction: SkyEye 7.3.6(3) measures FEATURE-MATCHING error under
    lighting mismatch, not seam appearance under a blend. It does not support
    this item. The item stands on its own.

P3. TEMPORAL METRICS -- SPLIT THREE WAYS, NOT SkyEye's SINGLE SCALAR.
    We have no temporal metric; overlap PSNR is static. But SkyEye's jitter
    metric (Eqs. 9-10) is not the drop-in rev 1 claimed:
      - Under poseSource = GroundTruth with estimators off, Hhat == H, so H_err
        is identity and it reads exactly zero. It scores the ESTIMATORS, not the
        pipeline.
      - It lumps together three mechanisms with different causes and different
        fixes. Report them separately:
            (a) winner-label churn fraction -- a patch popping to another
                drone's exposure and detail while every warp is perfectly
                stable. Invisible to a homography-residual metric.
            (b) canvas origin/axis delta -- centre-drone re-election rigidly
                shifts the whole mosaic. SkyEye's metric reports this as
                enormous uniform jitter, indistinguishable from per-view noise.
            (c) per-view residual jitter -- the only one SkyEye's scalar
                actually measures.
    Only (c) needs GT; (a) and (b) are free and are the artifacts an HMD wearer
    actually notices.
    Units: metres-on-surface is right for a mapping product. For an HMD the
    pilot-relevant quantity is angular error in the headset, and for seam
    visibility it is canvas pixels -- both already tracked. Keep MGRAPH's
    report-mean-AND-std discipline; _overlap_psnr already reports the WORST pair
    rather than the mean, which is the same instinct.

P4. TREAT THE REFINER'S OUTPUT AS A MEASUREMENT-QUALITY READ-OUT ONLY.
    Rev 1 proposed a "cheap analogue" of SkyEye's deformation coefficient C_d.
    It is not the same measurement and must not carry the same name.
    C_d works because F_n (raw RANSAC) and F_o (sensor-rectified RANSAC) are two
    INDEPENDENT modalities -- the ratio means something precisely because one
    side never touches the sensor. Correlating each view against a leave-one-out
    consensus warped BY THE POSES BEING ASSESSED has no independent modality
    left, only relative disagreement with ambiguous attribution:
        one bad pose        -> that view's residual rises           (wanted)
        plane wrong         -> every residual rises, misreads as
                               fleet-wide sensor failure
        untextured overlap  -> low peak ratio, misreads as sensor failure --
                               and the contrast / REFINE_MIN_PEAK_RATIO gates
                               already exist to call that NO MEASUREMENT
    Worst, the consensus is a MEAN, so its breakdown point is zero: at N=3 with
    one bad view, the two good views each correlate against a consensus
    containing the bad one, and the bad drone can score no worse than the clean
    ones. A median consensus is a prerequisite for the number to mean anything.
    Rev 1 also overstated readiness: the peak ratio exists, but no per-view
    residual variance is computed anywhere. That is new work, not "90% built".
    SCOPE: log it, never act on it, never label it telemetry health.

P5. CROSS-DRONE TRIANGULATION FOR THE PLANE -- CONDITIONAL, ALONGSIDE ACQUIRE.
    Demoted from rev 1's #1. Restored to exactly what CLAUDE.md already says it
    is: "the next thing to build IF ACQUIRE proves too slow or too easily
    fooled." No measurement has established that it has. Three reasons the
    rev-1 framing ("replace ACQUIRE") was wrong:
      - It puts vision on the acquisition critical path in the scene class the
        module exists to serve. Section 2 rejects both papers partly because
        vision collapses on low texture; PLANAR exists so there is "no failure
        on asphalt/water/uniform facades". Replacing ACQUIRE removes the
        fallback in exactly that case.
      - Sparse matching escapes the BASIN, not the DEGENERACY. A correspondence
        between views i and j constrains relative geometry only, so (wrong d,
        correct poses) and (correct d, convergently-wrong poses) fit the same
        match set exactly -- the identifiability nullspace planar_selftest.py
        already pins. The 1-D RANSAC in d is an elegant reduction and inherits
        it whole.
      - It assumes the plane NORMAL is right. In the field that comes from
        snapNormalToFormation or an operator. Normal error is not absorbable by
        d, so it appears as irreducible residual and RANSAC begins rejecting
        good matches.
    If built: supplement, keep ACQUIRE as fallback, gate on texture, and note it
    targets the LEAST sensitive term in the budget.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. REJECTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From the papers:

- MGRAPH's multi-graph + graph fusion. Solves out-of-order arrival and
  disconnected coverage; we have a simultaneous C#-chosen selection and no
  persistent map. Added state and a new failure mode for zero gain.

- Cascaded relative homographies + BA to remove the resulting drift. Ours are
  absolute by construction; the cascade would manufacture the drift Ceres then
  undoes.

- SkyEye's reference-frame and adjacency-frame schemes. Both amortize
  feature-matching cost we do not pay. Freezing a reference frame is
  structurally the "frozen snapshot poisons the recovery" failure the module
  already documents. Their STABILITY argument is sound and we reached it
  independently (centre-drone hysteresis; TRACK refusing to move without
  measurable improvement).

- M = R^-1 K^-1 orthorectify-then-similarity. Strictly weaker than G: discards
  metric position and scale, needs a reference, horizontal-plane only.

- The 4-DoF similarity as the PRIMARY warp. Valid only for gyrostabilized nadir
  at altitude; vertical-plane mode violates it outright.

From rev 1 of this document, dropped on review:

- CONFIDENCE-GATED DROPPING OF VIEWS (rev-1 item 3, wrongly called
  highest-value). Under NEAREST, demoting a view does not hand its pixels to a
  better neighbour -- the runner-up may not cover that plane region at all, so
  the result is a HOLE. Every other decision in the module resolves this trade
  the other way ("a frozen panorama beats a vanished one"; the border falloff
  MULTIPLIES the obliquity score rather than gating). A view with 60 px of error
  still shows real imagery of that facade, displaced 3.5 m. SkyEye can afford a
  threshold because puzzle-stitching chooses among alternatives over a batch;
  a Voronoi partition has one incumbent per pixel. And since the available
  confidence signal is texture-dominated (P4), the concrete effect is dropping
  coverage over blank facade -- where the geometry is still exact and no other
  evidence exists. It is also untestable today: PLANAR cannot run on the DJI
  path, and under sim GroundTruth there is nothing to demote.

- JOINT SOLVE REPLACING THE LEAVE-ONE-OUT ITERATION (rev-1 item 4). MGRAPH
  Eq. 5 minimizes reprojection over point correspondences; we have none, so
  "take the cost function shape" means either N^2 phase correlations (28 instead
  of 8 at N=8) or a nonlinear pixel-domain solve -- on the warp thread that
  shares one GPU and one GIL with the render loop. Further: "converges in fewer
  passes" is not a benefit, since refine_rate damping and TRACK's
  refuse-to-move-without-improvement are deliberate temporal stability for an
  HMD, so faster convergence is either low-passed away or buys jitter. The gauge
  freedom does NOT become a constraint -- a relative-only cost has the identical
  nullspace, and the re-gauging bookkeeping exists because MEMBERSHIP changes
  between passes, which a per-pass joint solve also has. The current
  one-vs-rest-with-accumulation is Gauss-Seidel on that same objective and
  converges to the same fixed point under stable membership; the gain is a
  step-count constant, not a different answer. (The footprint-based pair
  selection sub-point was correct but solves a problem only this item creates --
  the refiner's shared-coverage mask already restricts to genuine overlap.)

- WIDENING THE PER-VIEW CORRECTION TO INCLUDE ROTATION (rev-1 item 5). The
  motivation did not match the proposal: keystone is the PROJECTIVE part, and
  in-plane rotation represents none of it. The error-to-artifact mapping was
  also wrong -- at a vertical facade, yaw bias gives lateral shift plus a
  horizontal scale gradient, and gimbal-pitch bias gives vertical shift plus
  vertical keystone. In-plane patch rotation is what ROLL error produces, and
  gimbal roll is the best-stabilized axis and is not in the budget. Mechanically
  it is not a translation-space extension either: dpose is a correction to the
  camera CENTRE in world metres, so rotation means dR, which _phase_shift cannot
  measure (one rFFT gives translation only; rotation needs log-polar or
  iteration). And rev 1 accepted the degeneracy argument for keeping scale
  global without applying it to rotation, which over limited overlap has a short
  angular lever arm and trades directly against neighbours' translations.
