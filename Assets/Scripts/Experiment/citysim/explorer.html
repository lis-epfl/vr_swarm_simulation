Swarm Density Study

Which cities make the swarm better, or worse, than a single drone at spotting one person in a hat among sixty-four walkers? The study generated cities with controlled clutter and goal layouts, predicted what each interface sees with four tiers of model, and picked three trial levels with a rule fixed in advance. These are the three it picked.

What the codes mean

Every city the study generated is one clutter level joined to one goal geometry, written C·-G·. Three of those combinations became the trial levels L1, L2 and L3.

All 24 levels the simulation tried

Each cell is one clutter level (row) with one goal geometry (column). The clutter levels: how built-up the city is

Blocks are irregular, generated around random seed points, with streets between them. The buildings are one design from the ScaledCity pack at twice its size, 50 m tall. The maps show seed 0 of each level with today's goal geometry.

The goal geometries: what stands at each goal

Every city has three goals. At each one a central building, the occluder, is ringed by patrolled buildings. Eight walkers circle each patrolled building, and one of all the walkers wears the hat. Drawn to one scale, from real generated goals. The dashed circle is the clearance: how far from the centre the nearest other building stands.

Reading the map

North is up. The swarm starts at the triangle, west of the city, and visits the three goals. The small rectangles are buildings and the bands between the blocks are streets. At each goal the largest building in the dashed clearing is the occluder; the ones round it are patrolled by walkers.

What ran

The replica against Unity

The 2-D replica flew 17 recorded Unity flights per city again, from the same start with the same pilot. It passes when the median track error is within 10% of the path, the spacing within 15%, and the drones lost within 2 per 10 flights of Unity's. How a drone that hits a building is treated decided it.

Watch it happen: the L3 flight at spread 0.4 shows the replica beside Unity.

The replica on the pilot paths

Every pilot-path number rests on the replica, so it was checked again on those paths before any was used: Unity flew the swarm's and the single drone's goal flights in L1, L3 and the two porous cities (three cities each, twelve swarm flights per kind of city), every flight recorded, and the replica flew them again. Same limits as above, pooled per kind of city.

The transit measures, replica against Unity

The same Unity flights and the replica's re-flights of them (two each), scored by the study's transit measures at one altitude and pitch, so only the flying differs. Each cell is Unity → replica. ✕ marks a difference of more than 15% and more than two standard errors.

How far a goal is seen from (internal_3)

The discovery co-primary counts a goal as seen when its walkers are on a shown feed within 80 m. A pilot with no map cannot turn onto a goal before seeing it, so on the legs where internal_3's pilots visibly turned onto the goal for the final approach, first sight should come first. It matched best at about 200 m, which is why 200 m is reported beside 80 m.

internal_3: replayed, then predicted

The recorded human runs, scored through the study's camera model, reproduce the analysis to 0.04 s. Predicting them with Tier B's machinery gets the swarm's displayed exposure right and under-predicts the single drone and the head-visible feeds: the pilots did better than the scripted model.
