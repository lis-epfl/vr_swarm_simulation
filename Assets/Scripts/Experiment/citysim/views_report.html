S12 · the views study · proposal for review

Distributed Views

Does the swarm find the hats faster than one drone with the trials' camera, and faster than one drone whose 360 camera is shown on a sphere round the pilot? The second comparison is what the swarm's spread-out viewpoints add beyond seeing every way at once, and it is what the next human trials will measure.

    Decision 1

    The goal is the trials' whole task, not a point

    Fly internal_4's task: three goals drawn from the city's own buildings as the trial scene draws them at every Play, each a building ringed by buildings whose pedestrians walk round them, one pedestrian in a hat; the pilot does not know where any goal is; the clock runs from the start line to the press on the third hat.

    At a goal the two all-round views differ most. A 360 camera at one point sees the ring buildings on its own side, and the centre building hides the rest. The swarm, wrapped round the centre building as internal_4's swarm pilots flew it, looks out at every ring building at once, each from nearer. A point hidden at random would measure only the search between goals, which S2 and S3 have measured already.

    Each goal's time is split at its zone (the patch grown by 15 m, as analyse.py splits the trials) into between goals and at the goal, so the answer says where the difference comes from.

    Decision 2

    A hat is recognised at about 0.5 per second up close, half that at 31 m, almost never past 50 m

    Measured on internal_4's 14 runs, every 0.1 s: the hat's distance from the nearest camera that had it in the central 80% of a shown image, at the recorded gimbal pitch, with a clear line through the buildings, and inside the headset's ±50° of where the head pointed. That is the recorder's own test, measured instead of thresholded. Time on screen before a press is exposure; the press, credited to the hat's distance 1 s before it, is an event.

    Decision 3

    A goal's crowd is noticed at about 0.1 per second out to some 240 m

    On every leg between goals: the distance within which at least one of the goal's walkers was expected on the head's screens, and the moment the pilot turned onto the goal for the final approach (from there on the course stayed within 45° of it). Legs that began already on the final approach had noticed the crowd during the previous goal's search, so they are censored rather than dropped.

    Decision 4

    Each condition is flown the way internal_4's pilots flew it

    Between goals every condition walks the streets (internal_4's pilots flew over them 95–100% of the time), persistently and towards ground it has not seen, until it notices a crowd, then takes the street route there. At a goal the three differ, as the recorded tracks do: the swarm flies to the centre building and holds there so the formation wraps it, then circles the goal's block; the single drone works round the ring buildings one by one, orbiting some with the camera turned in on them and passing others; the sphere walks the goal's own junctions while the pilot looks round. Heads are internal_4's recorded traces, replayed.
