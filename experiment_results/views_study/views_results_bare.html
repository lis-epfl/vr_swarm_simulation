Study S12 · Distributed Views · for the human trials

Does the swarm find the hats faster than one drone with a 360 camera?

The swarm differs from one drone in two ways at once: its ten cameras face every way, and they stand in ten places. One drone whose 360 camera is shown on a sphere round the pilot has the first without the second, so the swarm against that drone measures what the spread-out viewpoints add beyond seeing every way at once. That is the next human comparison.

S12 flies the trials' whole task (three goals, a walking hat at each, from the start line to the third press) in the trial city, with scripted pilots on the 2D replica checked against Unity. How far away a hat and a crowd can be seen is fitted to internal_4's pilots.

Read this first: the 360 drone here is held back by resolution, not by its viewpoint. The main study shows the 360 camera as a real drone's 2K live stream (the Antigravity A1's). That is about 6 pixels per degree, a third of what a feed screen gives and a quarter of the full-resolution sphere. A hat that is 20 m away looks as small as one 60 m away on a feed. Shown at full resolution, the 360 drone ties the swarm. The study's headline 0.61× is therefore a verdict on the stream, not on 360 cameras.
