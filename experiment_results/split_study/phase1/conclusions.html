Findings · 28 Oct 2026

  • Phase 3: surrounding is what looks down a block's streets, and d_ref sets how far, but it still finds a goal more slowly. Scored on looking down each exit to the building at its end, the swarm in the streets knows 41% of a block's exits at p 40 and 9% at p 75; a 12 s surround at d_ref 1.8 looks down 93% and 17%. The wider the spread, the more it sees, until its drones touch the building it surrounds (above 1.8; 2.2 at p 75), which is wider than the trials' dial. Searching for a goal at a building, the surround took ×1.48 (1.18–1.87) the time of the swarm in the streets: it saved a few decisions, and each block took longer. See the Look down tab.
  • Surrounding a block shows more of its streets, but finds targets no sooner. A 6 s surround previews 95.5% of a block's streets when the pilot commits, 7.1 points more than a pause of the same length at the corner (H1 holds: 48 cities, p ≈ 2×10⁻³⁴). An informed pilot using that knowledge finds a hidden target in the same time as the swarm kept in the streets (H2 is not supported: ×1.02, 0.92–1.12). It takes 10% fewer blocks, but each block takes 21% longer.
  • The swarm's cameras are the effect. Kept in the streets, the swarm finds a target in 62% of one drone's time, and 90% of targets within 20 minutes against one drone's 80%. A single drone that stops to look down each street gains nothing (×1.05).
  • The pass round gives the preview for free, on Phase 2's measure. Flowing round the block on the way through previews 98% of its streets at no cost in time (×0.97 in the search), with more contacts than staying in the streets. Phase 3 qualifies it: the pass decides at the corner, so at its decision it has seen only what the swarm in the streets has, and by the next junction it has looked down about as much.
  • The surround is the costly way to take a block: about ten times the drones lost (0.29 against 0.03 per 10-minute walk, nearly all drone-drone collisions), and a quarter of its screens with no linked neighbour, against 7% in the streets.
  • Phase 1 chose variant R, and only p 40. One catalogue building per block, with Phase 2 on 40 m blocks with 18 and 24 m streets, the only designs where both manoeuvres passed every criterion fixed in advance. At larger blocks the surround needs 9–20 s or more and the pass round strands a drone. With block-shaped buildings (B) the drones were pressed into the building they flowed round (not, as first reported, the buildings across the street).
  • The coupling holds: the separation that surrounds a block is its ring perimeter ÷ 100. Cohesion's range is best at 30: at 40 the pull across a block drags drones into the building.
  • For you to decide: which screen-linkage measure stands for "the pilot can relate the screens" (the Screens tab gives all of them), and whether another full study is worth running. Phase 3 flew surrounds matched to the block in spread and length, and they looked down far more but still found a goal more slowly. A full study would need the look-down measure, d_ref as a factor beyond the dial, more cities, and probably a task where seeing down every exit before choosing matters more than it does in a search.

Checked in Unity: Phase 1's 480 bench flights agree with the replica on track, spacing, losses, preview, reform and envelopment. Phase 2's walks flown in Unity reproduce every contrast the hypotheses rest on (H1 +6.6 points against the replica's +6.8; the time-to-find ratios within 0.3%), and the 40 search routes found their targets within 5 s of the replica. Unity lost more drones (29 against 9 in 128 swarm walks) and touched buildings more, so the replica understates the costs.
