What this means for the next study

  • Score looking down, not entering. On the old preview the swarm in the streets looked nearly as informed as the surround at p 40 (71% of a block's exits against 95%). Looking down to the building at the street's end separates them: 41% against 93% for a 12 s surround at d_ref 1.8. At p 75 the street-bound swarm looks down 9%. A full study should make looking down its primary preview measure and keep entering and seeing to the end as levels.
  • Study d_ref as a factor, and widen the dial for it. With 18 m streets the surround looks down more the wider it flies, up to 1.8 (2.2 at p 75), where drones start touching the building they surround. That is past the trials' 0.4–1.6 dial, and past Phase 1's d* at p 40–60. If people are to fly the surround, BlockCityWorld's dial has to reach it (the RC bridge's axis range), with the streets kept at 1.0.
  • A surround has to be long enough for its block. At d_ref 1.8 a 12 s surround looks down 93% of a block's exits at p 40 and 79% at p 50; at p 60 it looks down half, and 20 s about two-thirds. At p 75 no safe setting looks down even half with ten drones (38% at best). The 1.5 time price holds the rule to 6–12 s. A full study should fly the longer surround as a condition of its own rather than let a time bar choose against it.
  • The pass is not a free look. It decides at the corner, before it goes round, and by the next junction it has looked down about what the street-bound swarm has.
  • Looking down does not find a goal at a building sooner. In Part B's search the surround the rule picked took ×1.48 (1.18–1.87) the time of the swarm kept in the streets: ×1.27 at p 40 and ×1.74 at p 60. It needed 3–6% fewer decisions, and each took 27–74% longer. What it sees down an exit before choosing, the street-bound swarm sees one block later by flying the exit it chose. It did beat a pause of the same length at the corner at p 40 (×0.83), and the rule's wider spread did no better than Phase 2's surround (×1.06). The surround could still pay where the decision itself is the cost: a pilot who must commit without flying down an exit, or a goal that has to be confirmed from more than one side. Neither was flown.
  • Before a full run. More cities: Part A's intervals are over trials in three cities, and Part B's eight cities per design give a pilot's width. Street widths 12 and 24 m too: the contacts that cap the spread are with the building being surrounded, so whether the safe spread moves with the street is open. A Unity bench check of the wide-spread surround, since PhysX may resolve those contacts differently. And a per-decision log in any adaptive pilot from the start (lesson 32).
