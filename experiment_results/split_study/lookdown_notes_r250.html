What this means for the next study (at a 250 m sight range)

Phase 3 was flown again on 2026-10-28 with a camera counting as seeing within 250 m rather than 80 m, at the user's request: 80 m was too short to mean anything. Every flight is the same; only what counts as seen changed. Part B's surround was picked again by the same rule, from Part A at 250 m.

  • At 250 m the swarm in the streets already looks down most of a block's exits. At the decision it has looked down 87% at p 40 (41% at 80 m), 75% at p 50, 63% at p 60 and 51% at p 75. A 12 s surround at d_ref 1.8 looks down 99 / 97 / 92 / 81%, so the surround's lead is 12 points on the smallest blocks and 30 on the largest. One drone looks down 33 / 33 / 27 / 23%.
  • The spread matters much less for seeing at this range. A 12 s surround looks down 99% at p 40 at every spread from 1.0 to 2.6, 89–95% at p 60 and 80–82% at p 75. The widest safe spread is still 1.8 (2.2 at p 75): it is set by drones touching the building they surround, and the formation still needs it to enclose the block.
  • Longer surrounds see nearly everything. At d_ref 1.8 a 20 s surround looks down 99 / 98 / 99 / 92%. The rule picks a 6 s firm hold on the block at p 40 (96%), a 6 s aim-and-turn at p 50 (91%), a 12 s aim-and-turn at p 60 (92%) and a 12 s aim-and-turn at d_ref 1.4 at p 75 (82%).
  • The pass is still not a free look. By the next junction it has looked down 100 / 96 / 93 / 91% of the block's exits, against the street-bound swarm's 99 / 97 / 95 / 90%.
  • Looking down still does not find a goal at a building sooner. In Part B the surround took ×1.26 (1.01–1.58) the time of the swarm kept in the streets: ×1.21 at p 40 (0.83–1.75) and ×1.32 at p 60 (1.00–1.74). It needed 15% fewer decisions, and each took 32% (p 40) to 60% (p 60) longer. It beat a pause of the same length at the corner only by ×0.89 (0.72–1.10). The swarm in the streets found the goal in 51% of one drone's time. Phase 2's search, flown again at 250 m, agrees: the surround took ×1.08 (0.97–1.21) the street-bound swarm's time.
  • Before a full run. As at 80 m: more cities (Part B's eight per design give a pilot's width), 12 and 24 m streets, a Unity bench check of the wide-spread surround, and a per-decision log in any adaptive pilot from the start (lesson 32). The surround could still pay where the decision itself is the cost: a pilot who must commit without flying down an exit, or a goal that has to be confirmed from more than one side. Neither was flown.
